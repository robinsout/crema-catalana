1. La festa de final de curs: speeches and congratulations

The last unit of Bàsic 1 is a wrap-up. There's almost no new grammar: at the end-of-course party the mayor welcomes the students, the local radio does interviews, and the students talk about themselves and each other. You can already do each of these things separately — here they all come together in one conversation. First, the words of the party itself.

Festa de final de curs. Divendres, 19 de juny, a les sis de la tarda, a la sala d'actes. Berenar per a tothom! — End-of-course party. Friday 19 June, 6 p.m., in the main hall. Afternoon snack for everyone!

  • La sala d'actes — the main hall, assembly hall. El berenar — the afternoon snack, a light bite between lunch and a late dinner (lesson for unit 4, chapter 2): at the party, that means pastries, coca (a flat sweet cake) and drinks.
  • Per a tothom — “for everyone”. Tothom means “everyone, everybody”, and just like in English the verb after it is singular: Tothom parla català. — Everyone speaks Catalan. Not tothom parlen.

1.1 How to address the room

A party speech is built from set phrases. They're worth knowing both to follow what's said and to say a few words yourself at the end of the course:

CatalanEnglishNote
Bona tarda a tots i a totes.Good afternoon (good evening), everyone.in public speeches both genders are named: tots (men, or a mixed group), totes (women); before lunch — Bon dia
Benvinguts i benvingudes!Welcome!it's an adjective, so it agrees: benvingut to a man, benvinguda to a woman
Per començar, em presento.To begin with, let me introduce myself.
Felicitats a tots!Congratulations, everyone!also “happy birthday”
Enhorabona!Congratulations! Well done!for an achievement: you passed an exam, finished the course
Moltes gràcies a la professora.Many thanks to our teacher.
Bon estiu i fins al curs que ve!Have a good summer, and see you next year!el curs que ve — next school year (literally “the course that comes”)

Pronunciation

In enhorabona the h is silent and the unstressed vowels are reduced: [ənuɾəˈβɔnə]. In felicitats the stress falls on the last syllable, as in all words ending in -at without a written accent: [fəlisiˈtats].

1.2 L'Ajuntament, l'alcalde

Adult courses are often run by the town, so the mayor speaks at the party.

  • L'Ajuntament — the town hall, the city council, and the building itself. In small towns and villages it's called la casa de la vila — literally “the town house” (la vila — small town). Barcelona has its Casa de la Ciutat too: that's the name of the city hall building on Plaça de Sant Jaume.
  • L'alcalde — the mayor, l'alcaldessa — a woman mayor (the -essa ending, as in metgessa in the lesson for unit 6). El regidor, la regidora — a councillor in charge of one area: la regidora de cultura.
  • In Ajuntament the final t after n is silent: [əʒuntəˈmen]. The same in el dependent, l'estudiant.

1.3 The school year: inscripcions, cursos, exàmens

On the radio, the head of the school usually explains how the year works. The words will be useful for your own course too:

  • La sortida — here “an outing, a group trip”, not only “the exit”. Fer una sortida al museu — to go to the museum as a group.
  • L'examen is masculine — learn it with the article, since l' hides the gender. In the plural it takes a written accent: els exàmens. Aprovar — to pass, suspendre — to fail: He aprovat! — I passed!
  • Fer vacances — to be on holiday, like fer festa — to have a day off. The noun is always plural: les vacances, even though English says on holiday.

False friends

Suspendre an exam is to fail it — nobody is suspended. Aprovar is to pass, not just “approve”. La conferència is usually a single talk or lecture, not a whole conference.

1.4 Em dic Joaquim, però em diuen Quim

The mayor in the textbook introduces himself with his full name and then adds what people actually call him. Two structures to tell apart:

Em dic Joaquim. — My name is Joaquim (the name on my documents).
Em diuen Quim. — People call me Quim (literally “they call me”).

Em dic is the reflexive verb dir-se, “to be called”, literally “I call myself” (lesson for unit 1, chapter 2). Em diuen is plain dir in the 3rd person plural: “they call me” — exactly the English phrase. About someone else: Es diu Francesc, però li diuen Cesc. — His name is Francesc, but they call him Cesc.

Catalan nicknames keep one or two syllables of the full name — often the last ones (Quim, Cesc, Txell), sometimes the beginning (Xavi, Montse). They're worth recognising, because in real life that's what you'll hear:

For English speakers

English names with th, w or a silent letter can be hard for Catalans to say. Offer a short form yourself: Em dic Matthew, però em diuen Matt. Or simply: Em pots dir Matt. — You can call me Matt.

2. Vostè: asking questions politely

On the course everyone uses tu from day one. But in a radio interview, with the mayor, with older people, at the doctor's and in offices you'll hear vostè — the polite “you” (lesson for unit 1, chapter 3). English has only one you; Catalan has two, and the polite one takes the 3rd-person verb, as if you said “Is sir from here?”. That's history: vostè comes from vostra mercè, “your grace”. Vostè és d'aquí? — Are you from here?

Everything that changes from tu to vostè shows up in these pairs of questions:

TuVostèEnglish
Ets d'aquí?Vostè és d'aquí?Are you from here?
On vius?On viu?Where do you live?
Vius sol?Viu sol?Do you live alone?
Tens fills?Té fills?Do you have children?
Com et dius?Com es diu?What's your name?
A què et dediques?A què es dedica?What do you do?
Quin horari fas?Quin horari fa?What hours do you work?
T'agrada la teva feina?Li agrada la seva feina?Do you like your job?
Què fas el cap de setmana?Què fa el cap de setmana?What do you do at the weekend?
  • The verb goes into the 3rd person: ets → és, vius → viu, tens → té, fas → fa.
  • The reflexive et becomes es: et dius → es diu, et lleves → es lleva.
  • “To you” et becomes li: t'agrada → li agrada (more in chapter 4).
  • “Your” becomes el seu, la seva: la teva feina → la seva feina.

One form, three meanings

Viu sola? means both “Do you live alone?” (to a woman, polite) and “Does she live alone?”. That's why vostè is said out loud at the start of a conversation — Vostè és d'aquí? — and then dropped once it's clear who you're talking to. In the same way la seva feina is “your job”, “his job” or “her job”.

If you know Spanish

Vostè works exactly like usted: 3rd-person verb, el seu, la seva for “your”, and it's dropped once it's clear.

2.1 Senyor, senyora and polite phrases

  • When you speak to someone you call vostè, use senyor or senyora + surname, like Mr / Mrs, with no article: Bon dia, senyora Vidal. But when you talk about them, the article is needed — literally “the Mrs Vidal”: La senyora Vidal és la directora.
  • If the other person wants to switch to tu, they'll say: Em pot dir Montse. — You can call me Montse. Or: Ens podem tractar de tu? — Shall we use tu? In Catalonia people switch to tu quickly.
  • Polite requests use the vostè form: Perdoni (sorry, excuse me), Escolti (excuse me — to get attention, literally “listen”), Disculpi. With tu: perdona, escolta, disculpa.
  • To end an interview: Moltes gràcies pel seu temps. — Thank you very much for your time. The answer: A vostè. — Thank you. (With tu: A tu.)

Pronunciation

Vostè is [busˈtɛ]: v sounds like [b] in central Catalan, unstressed o is [u] (as in put), and stressed è is open, like the e in bed. The plural is vostès [busˈtɛs], with a 3rd-person plural verb: Vostès són d'aquí? — Are you (all) from here?

3. Parlar de mi: des de fa, fa … que

When you talk about yourself, you almost always say how long something has been going on: how long you've lived here, how long you've been learning Catalan, how long you've had this job. Catalan has two equally common structures for this:

StructureExampleEnglish
… des de fa + periodVisc a Barcelona des de fa tres anys.I've lived in Barcelona for three years.
Fa + period + que …Fa tres anys que visc a Barcelona.I've lived in Barcelona for three years.

Both mean “I've lived in Barcelona for three years (and still do)”. Note the verb: English uses have lived, have been learning, but Catalan uses the plain Present — visc, “I live”. Don't reach for the Perfet (he viscut) here: for something still going on, it's the Present. More examples:

Estudio català des de fa vuit mesos. — I've been studying Catalan for eight months.
Fa dos anys que treballo en aquesta empresa. — I've worked at this company for two years.
Fa molt de temps que no veig els meus pares. — I haven't seen my parents for a long time.
Som amigues des de fa vint anys. — We've been friends for twenty years.

3.1 Des de or des de fa: since or for

English already makes the distinction for you — just map it:

  • des de + a point in time = since: des del 2023 (since 2023 — years usually take the article; des de 2023 is also correct), des de dilluns (since Monday), des de l'estiu (since the summer), des de petit (since I was little).
  • des de fa + a period = for: des de fa tres anys (for three years), des de fa una setmana (for a week).
  • A typical mistake is des de fa el 2023 or des de tres anys. The check: if English says for, you need fa; if it says since, you don't. “Since I was three” (an age, not a period) is des dels tres anys.

There are two ways to ask, too:

Des de quan vius aquí? — Since when have you lived here? — Des del 2022. / Des de fa tres anys.
Quant de temps fa que estudies català? — How long have you been studying Catalan? — Fa un any.

Fa tres anys: “for” and “ago”

With the Present, fa tres anys que… means “for three years”. With a past tense it means “three years ago”: Vaig arribar fa tres anys. — I arrived three years ago. (Yes, vaig looks like “I go”, but vaig arribar is a past tense, the Passat perifràstic; it comes in Bàsic 2 — overview in the lesson on tenses.) For now, just remember that fa + period can also mean “ago”.

If you know Spanish

Both structures are word for word Spanish: desde hace tres años, hace tres años que… — just with fa for hace.

3.2 Talking about yourself, step by step

A short introduction at the party or in an interview usually goes in this order — and you already have a lesson for every step:

  1. Em dic Catherine, però em diuen Kate. — your name (unit 1)
  2. Soc d'Anglaterra, de Manchester. La meva llengua materna és l'anglès. — where you're from and your language (unit 1)
  3. Visc a Barcelona des de fa dos anys, al barri de Gràcia. — where you live and for how long (unit 3)
  4. Visc amb el meu marit i el meu fill, que té set anys. — family (unit 2)
  5. Treballo de dissenyadora per a una empresa de Berlín. Teletreballo des de casa. — work (unit 6)
  6. Em llevo a les set i plego a les cinc. — your day and hours (unit 4, unit 5)
  7. M'agrada molt la meva feina. El que no m'agrada gaire és que passo moltes hores davant de l'ordinador. — what you like and don't (chapter 4)

Que té set anys — “who is seven” (literally “who has seven years”: age is tenir, not “to be”). Que covers English who, which, that and never changes for gender or number. Teletreballo des de casa — I work from home.

4. M'agrada, no m'agrada gaire: what you like

Agradar works the other way round from English like. Catalan doesn't say “I like coffee” but “coffee pleases me”: m'agrada el cafè. The thing you like is the subject, and the person who likes it is the object. So the verb agrees with the thing: one thing or an activity — agrada, several things — agraden.

Who likes itOne thing, an activitySeveral things
(a mi) Im'agrada el cafèm'agraden les festes
(a tu) yout'agrada ballar?t'agraden els gats?
(a ell, a ella, a vostè) he, she, you (polite)li agrada la seva feinali agraden els nens
(a nosaltres) weens agrada el marens agraden les sortides
(a vosaltres) you (all)us agrada el curs?us agraden els exàmens?
(a ells, a elles, a vostès) they, you (polite, plural)els agrada cuinarels agraden les plantes
  • With an infinitive it's always singular, even with several activities: M'agrada llegir i passejar. — I like reading and walking.
  • Before a vowel em, et shorten to m', t' (and agradar always starts with a vowel), while ens, us, els, li don't change.
  • “They like” is els agrada. In speech you may hear lis agrada, but it's a mistake.
  • For emphasis or contrast, add a mi, a tu, a ella…: A mi m'agrada el te, i a ella li agrada el cafè. — I like tea, and she likes coffee. Note: mi has no accent.

4.1 How much you like it

CatalanEnglish
M'encanta!I love it!
M'agrada molt.I like it a lot.
M'agrada bastant.I quite like it.
No m'agrada gaire.I'm not too keen on it.
No m'agrada gens.I don't like it at all.

Gaire (“much”) and gens (“at all”) in a statement only go with no (more in the lesson for unit 8). No m'agrada gaire is the soft, polite way to say you don't like something — like English it's not really my thing. Talking about someone else's job or town, it sounds much better than a flat no m'agrada. M'encanta works like m'agrada: m'encanten les festes — I love parties.

4.2 El que m'agrada és…

To say what exactly you like or don't, use el que — English what in “what I like is…”:

El que més m'agrada de Barcelona és el mar. — What I like most about Barcelona is the sea.
El que no m'agrada gaire és que plego tard. — What I'm not too keen on is that I finish late.
El que m'agrada de la meva feina és que conec molta gent. — What I like about my job is that I meet lots of people.

Not què

This what is not the question word què: Què més m'agrada és el mar is wrong. Què asks (Què t'agrada? — What do you like?); el que means “the thing that”. You'll also hear lo que m'agrada, even from Catalans — that's Spanish creeping in; Catalan has no article lo.

If you know Spanish

Agradar is gustar, with three traps: Spanish les gusta is els agrada; a mí is a mi, without an accent; and lo que is el que.

4.3 A mi també, a mi tampoc

You can react to someone else's “I like” in two words — like jo també, jo tampoc in the lesson for unit 2, but with a mi, because with agradar you're the one it pleases:

They sayAgreeingDisagreeing
M'agrada el futbol.A mi també. — Me too.A mi no. — I don't.
No m'agrada el fred.A mi tampoc. — Me neither.A mi sí. — I do.

English uses me too for everything; Catalan picks: jo també after an ordinary verb (Soc anglès. — Jo també.), a mi també after agradar.

The unit ends with students introducing each other to the group. For that, everything you'd say about yourself has to go into the 3rd person. The verb and the possessives change, and sometimes the pronoun too:

  • El seu, la seva, els seus, les seves agree with the thing owned, not with the owner: la seva dona — his wife, el seu marit — her husband, els seus fills — his or her children (lesson for unit 1, chapter 5; unit 2, chapter 4).
  • Countries that take an article keep it: és del Marroc, del Perú, del Brasil, de l'Equador, dels Estats Units — English does this only with a few, like the United States.
  • The first time, use the personal article and the name: en Karim, la Valentina, l'Amina; after that ell, ella or no subject at all.

5.1 Us presento en Karim

To introduce someone to a group, say Us presento… — “let me introduce… to you” (us — to you all). To one person: Et presento…; with vostè: Li presento…

Us presento en Karim. — Let me introduce Karim.
Aquesta és la Valentina, la meva companya. — This is Valentina, my classmate.
Encantat de conèixer-te! / Encantada! — Nice to meet you! (encantat if you're a man, encantada if you're a woman)

No preposition before the person

English introduces a bare name: This is Karim. I know Maria. Catalan has no preposition either — just, as always, the personal article before the name: Et presento en Joan. Conec la Maria. Not et presento a en Joan.

If you know Spanish

Spanish puts a before a person: te presento a Juan, conozco a María. Catalan doesn't: no preposition, just the personal article, as before any name: Et presento en Joan.

For English speakers

Retelling in English, you already change I live → he lives, my → his. In Catalan there are two extra traps. First, pick seu or seva by the thing, not the person: Valentina's husband is el seu marit, not la seva marit. Second, don't forget the pronouns with reflexive verbs and agradar: em llevo → es lleva, m'agrada → li agrada. The most common mistake is leaving m'agrada or el meu in a story about someone else.
