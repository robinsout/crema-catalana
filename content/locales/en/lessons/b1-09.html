1. Què portes a la bossa? Bag, backpack, pockets

This unit is about everyday things: what's in your bag and pockets, whose it is, what it's for and what it's like. The key verb is portar — “to carry, to have on you”:

Porto les claus a la butxaca. — I've got the keys in my pocket.
Què portes a la bossa? — What have you got in your bag?
Avui no porto paraigua. — I haven't got an umbrella today.

  • Where English says “I've got … on me / with me”, Catalan says portar. The same verb also means “to bring” and “to wear” (porta ulleres — she wears glasses, lesson for unit 8).
  • Notice what's missing in the examples. “In my pocket” is a la butxaca — “in the pocket”: when it's obvious whose pocket it is, Catalan uses el, la, not el meu, la meva. And “an umbrella” has no article: with things you'd carry just one of, Catalan often drops un, una after no and in questions — Avui no porto paraigua, Portes paraigua? (have you got an umbrella?).
  • “In the bag” is a la bossa, with a — not en la bossa (lesson 3, chapter 2.1). “Inside the bag” is dins de la bossa or a dins de la bossa.
  • In Barcelona you'll also hear dur, which means the same: Què duus? — Duc les claus. It's worth recognising, but you can always say portar.

2. De qui és? — És meu

Catalan has no single word for “whose”. You ask de qui — “of whom”:

De qui és aquest mòbil? — Whose phone is this?
De qui són aquestes claus? — Whose keys are these?

The answer is a possessive without an article — the same forms as in lesson 1, chapter 5 and lesson 2, chapter 4, just without el, la, els, les. That's the “mine, yours” form:

De qui és aquest mòbil? — És meu. — It's mine.
I aquesta cartera? — És meva. — And this wallet? — It's mine.
De qui són aquests auriculars? Són teus? — Sí, són meus. — Whose earphones are these? Are they yours? — Yes, they're mine.
Aquestes claus són vostres? — No, no són nostres. — Are these keys yours (plural)? — No, they aren't ours.

English has one “mine” for everything. Catalan has four, and the form follows the thing, not the owner: Jordi says Són meves about his keys, because les claus is feminine plural.

2.1 És meu or és el meu

English has two words, my and mine. Catalan has one, meu, and the article does the work:

  • És meu. answers “whose is it?”: De qui és aquest paraigua? — És meu.
  • És el meu. answers “which one is yours?” when there are several: Quin és el teu paraigua? — Aquest és el meu. In English you'd say “this one” or “mine”; in Catalan el meu stands for “my one”.

You don't need to repeat the noun: Aquest abric no és el meu. El meu és vermell. — This coat isn't mine. Mine is red.

2.2 D'en Marc, de la Laia, d'ella

There's no 's: “Marc's keys” is “the keys of Marc”, with de + the personal article (lesson 2, chapter 5):

Les claus són d'en Marc. — They're Marc's keys.
La cartera és de la Laia. — The wallet is Laia's.
Les ulleres són de l'Oriol. — The glasses are Oriol's.
El llibre és del professor. — The book is the teacher's (de + el = del).

  • Seu, seva covers “his”, “hers”, “theirs” and polite “yours” (vostè) all at once: la seva bossa is his bag, her bag, their bag or your bag. Again it agrees with the bag, not with the person. To be clear, say d'ell, d'ella, d'ells: És de la Marta? — Sí, és d'ella. — Is it Marta's? — Yes, it's hers.
  • In speech you'll often hear del Marc instead of d'en Marc. In class and in writing use d'en.

3. Aquest i aquell, aquí i allà: this and that

Good news: central Catalan works like English, with two steps — near and far. You know aquest, aquesta (“this”) from lesson 2, chapter 3. Now add aquell (“that”) and the words for “here” and “there”. The difference from English: four forms each, for gender and number.

masc.fem.masc. pl.fem. pl.no nounwhere
nearaquestaquestaaquestsaquestesaixòaquí
faraquellaquellaaquellsaquellesallòallà

Aquest rellotge és meu i aquell és d'en Pau. — This watch is mine and that one is Pau's.
Aquesta bufanda és teva? — No, la meva és aquella d'allà, al penjador. — Is this scarf yours? — No, mine's that one over there, on the coat rack.
La teva cartera és aquí, a sobre de la taula. — Your wallet is here, on the table.

  • aquí — here, allà — there. There's also allí, but in Barcelona people mostly say allà.
  • “This one”, “that one” — just aquest, aquell with the right ending, no word for “one”. Add d'aquí, d'allà to point: aquest d'aquí — this one here, aquell d'allà — that one over there.
  • Where something is — ser: El mòbil és aquí. — The phone is here. Whether it exists at all — hi ha (“there is”, lesson 3, chapter 5). “Your wallet's here” is never hi ha aquí.
  • Pronunciation: aquell [əˈkeʎ], això [əˈʃɔ] (sh), allò [əˈʎɔ]. The first a is the weak vowel of about.

3.1 Això and allò: “this” and “that” on their own

When you don't name the thing — because you don't know what it is, or it doesn't matter — use això (near) and allò (far). They never change:

Què és això? — És un carregador. — What's this? — It's a charger.
I allò d'allà, què és? — És la paperera. — And what's that over there? — It's the bin.
Això és meu! — That's mine!

As soon as you name the thing, it's aquest or aquell again, with its gender: Aquest carregador és teu?

English “this”, “it”

English uses this and it for everything; Catalan picks the form:

  • “What's this?” about an unknown thing — Què és això?, not Què és aquest?
  • “This is my backpack”, pointing at it — Aquesta és la meva motxilla. (feminine, like motxilla) or simply És la meva motxilla. “It” disappears: no word for it.
  • “These are the keys” — the verb is plural, as in English: Aquestes són les meves claus. or Són les claus., not És les claus.

If you know Spanish

Spanish has three steps (este, ese, aquel); central Catalan has two. Aquí covers aquí and ahí, allà covers allí, allá. Some Valencian varieties (and old texts) have three steps: aquest, aqueix, aquell and açò, això, allò. Good to recognise, but in Barcelona people use two.

4. Per a què serveix? Servir per + infinitive

“What's it for?” is Per a què serveix? — literally “for what does it serve?”. English puts “for” at the end; Catalan puts per a at the start. The answer is serveix per + infinitive:

Per a què serveix aquesta motxilla? — Serveix per portar el portàtil i els llibres. — What's this backpack for? — It's for carrying my laptop and books.
Un mòbil serveix per trucar i enviar missatges. — A phone is for calling and sending messages.
Les claus serveixen per obrir i tancar les portes. — Keys are for opening and closing doors.
Una agenda serveix per apuntar coses. — A diary is for writing things down.

Where English says “for …ing”, Catalan uses the infinitive: per escriure — for writing, per obrir — for opening. Never the gerund (escrivint, obrint) — the form that looks like English -ing.

Servir is an -eix- verb (introductory lesson, chapter 5.2), but you mostly need two forms: serveix (it's for) and serveixen (they're for).

1serveixoservim

4.1 Per a or per; per a què or per què

  • “For, to whom” before a noun or pronoun — per a: Per a què?, Aquest regal és per a tu. (this present is for you), Serveix per a moltes coses. (it's useful for lots of things).
  • Before an infinitive — per: serveix per escriure, per no mullar-se (so as not to get wet). In written, more formal texts you'll also meet per a + infinitive — that's correct too.
  • Per a què? — what for? Per què? — why? They sound almost the same, so listen for the context. The answer to “why” is perquè (because), written as one word: Per què portes paraigua? — Perquè plou. — Why have you got an umbrella? — Because it's raining.
  • No serveix per a res. — It's no use at all (literally “it doesn't serve for nothing”, see chapter 7).

4.2 Per pentinar-te: “yourself” after the infinitive

Reflexive verbs carry their pronoun after the infinitive, with a hyphen (lesson 5, chapter 2.1). Talking in general — -se; talking to the person in front of you — -te:

Una pinta serveix per pentinar-se. — A comb is for combing your hair.
Té, això serveix per pentinar-te. — Here, you can comb your hair with this.
Un paraigua serveix per no mullar-se. — An umbrella stops you getting wet.
Un mocador serveix per mocar-se. — A handkerchief is for blowing your nose.
Les ulleres de sol serveixen per protegir-se els ulls. — Sunglasses are for protecting your eyes.

English says “your hair, your nose, your eyes”; Catalan says “the eyes” and lets -se show whose: protegir-se els ulls, rentar-se les mans — to wash your hands. Many verbs already include the body part: pentinar-se is “to comb your hair”, mocar-se is “to blow your nose”.

4.3 Fer servir: “to use”

The everyday verb for “to use” is fer servir — literally “to make serve”. Only fer changes:

Faig servir el mòbil per llegir. — I use my phone for reading.
Com es fa servir això? — How do you use this?
No faig servir mai l'agenda. — I never use my diary.

There's also utilitzar (spelt with tz), which sounds more formal.

5. Com és? Size, shape, material, colour

To ask what something is like, say Com és? — “What's it like?” (not “How is it?”: that's Com està?). More precise questions: De què és? — What's it made of? De quin color és? — What colour is it?

False friends

La roba is fabric and also clothes — not a robe: de roba — made of fabric. La pell is leather and skin (not “peel”). La fusta is wood as a material. Una ampolla is a bottle (not an ampoule). La goma (chapter 6) is a rubber, an eraser — not gum, which is el xiclet.

5.2 Several features at once

The features follow the noun one after another; parts of a thing are described with tenir (to have):

Busco el meu paraigua. És negre i plegable, i té el mànec de fusta. — I'm looking for my umbrella. It's a black folding one with a wooden handle.
Vull una cartera de pell marró, de mida mitjana. — I want a medium-sized brown leather wallet.
Porto unes ulleres de sol rodones i molt grosses. — I'm wearing round, very big sunglasses.

Compare the order: English stacks everything before the noun (a medium-sized brown leather wallet), Catalan puts it all after (una cartera de pell marró, de mida mitjana). And unes — the plural of una — is needed with plural-only things: unes ulleres — a pair of glasses.

Tens un bolígraf? — Have you got a pen?
Em deixes la goma, si us plau? — Can I borrow your rubber, please?
Té. — Here you are. (polite: Tingui.)
Com es diu això en català? — Es diu «guix». — What's this called in Catalan? — It's called “guix”.
Què vol dir «paperera»? — What does “paperera” mean?

English borrows; Catalan lends. Em deixes la goma? is literally “Do you lend me the rubber?” — deixar is “to lend”, with em (“me”) in front of the verb.

7. Algun, cap, algú, ningú, res

Some, any, no; somebody, nobody; something, nothing:

SomeAny / no
thing (+ noun)algun, alguna, alguns, algunes — some, anycap — no, not any
personalgú — somebody, anybodyningú — nobody, not anybody
thing (alone)alguna cosa — something, anythingres — nothing, not anything

Hi ha algun ordinador a l'aula? — No, no hi ha cap ordinador. — Is there a computer in the classroom? — No, there isn't a single one.
Hi ha alguna paperera? — Sí, al costat de la porta. — Is there a bin? — Yes, next to the door.
Hi ha algú a l'aula? — No, no hi ha ningú. — Is anybody in the classroom? — No, there's nobody.
Portes alguna cosa a les butxaques? — No, no porto res. — Have you got anything in your pockets? — No, nothing.

  • Double negative. After the verb you need no as well: No hi ha ningú. — literally “there isn't nobody”. In English that's a mistake; in Catalan it's the only correct way (lesson 5, chapter 5.1, the same as with mai). Think of ningú, res, cap as English “anybody, anything, any”: no … ningú — not … anybody. When the word comes before the verb, no is optional: Ningú (no) porta paraigua. — Nobody has an umbrella.
  • Cap never changes for gender or number: cap llibre, cap cadira. The noun after it is usually singular — “no book”, not “no books”.
  • Short answers are one word: Qui hi ha? — Ningú. (Nobody.) Què portes? — Res. (Nothing.) Quants llibres tens? — Cap. (None.)
  • algú [əlˈɣu] and ningú [niŋˈɡu] — stress on the last syllable, as the accent shows.

Cap, ningú, res in questions = “any”

In a question these words mean “any”, not “no” — exactly like English any, anybody, anything:

Tens cap pregunta? — Have you got any questions?
Hi ha ningú? — Is anybody there?
Vols res més? — Do you want anything else?

Tens alguna pregunta? is also correct. You can stick to algun, alguna yourself, but you'll hear both, especially Vols res més? in shops and bars.

  • De res. — You're welcome (reply to gràcies; literally “of nothing”).
  • Tothom — everybody, with a singular verb, as in English: Tothom porta mòbil. — Everybody has a phone.
  • Cap also means “head” (el cap), “boss”, “towards” (cap a casa — towards home), and it's in el cap de setmana — the weekend. Context tells them apart.

If you know Spanish

Cap is ningún, ninguna, ningú is nadie, res is nada. But Spanish can't use them in questions: Tens cap pregunta? is ¿tienes alguna pregunta?, not “ninguna”.

8. Butxaca → butxaques: plurals with a spelling change

Words ending in -a make the plural with -es (pronounced [əs]). Before e some letters would be read differently, so the spelling changes to keep the same sound — just as English writes picnic → picnicking to keep the hard c. In Catalan this happens all the time:

EndingExamplesWhy
-ca → -quesbutxaca → butxaques, blanca → blanquesce would read [sə]
-ga → -guesbotiga → botigues, groga → groguesge would read [ʒə]
-ja → -gesplatja → platges, taronja → tarongesbefore e the sound [ʒ] is spelt g
-ça → -cesplaça → places, peça → pecesbefore e the cedilla isn't needed
-gua → -güesparaigua → paraigües, llengua → llengüeswithout the dots the u in gue would be silent

The same spelling rule changes verb forms before e and i (introductory lesson, chapter 5.4).

  • El paraigua is masculine, but its plural follows this rule: els paraigües. The two dots on ü are the diaeresis (introductory lesson, chapter 2.3).
  • Masculine words stressed on the last syllable and ending in -s, -ç, -x, -tx add -os — an extra syllable, like English bus → buses, box → boxes: gros → grossos, el guix → els guixos, el braç → els braços, el pis → els pisos. Double ss or single s follows the sound: grossos has [s], pisos has [z], as in English busy (lesson for unit 8, chapter 3).
  • An unstressed final -s doesn't change: el llapis → els llapis, el pintallavis → els pintallavis — like English one sheep, two sheep.

9. Veig, veig, què veus? A guessing game

The unit's final task is the Catalan version of I spy. Instead of “something beginning with…”, you guess with questions. The rules:

  1. One player picks an object in the classroom and says Veig, veig. — I see, I see.
  2. The others ask Què veus? — What do you see? The answer is Una cosa. or Una coseta. — Something. / A little thing.
  3. Then only yes/no questions. No repeating a question.
  4. You can guess after five or six questions.

The verb veure (“to see”) is irregular:

És gros? És petit? — Is it big? Is it small?

Answers: Sí. No. Més o menys. (sort of) No ho sé. (I don't know)

A la motxilla porto el llibre de català i una llibreta de ratlles. També porto un estoig blau de roba amb dos bolígrafs i un llapis. A la butxaca de davant hi ha les claus de casa, la cartera i el mòbil. La meva cartera és petita i negra, i és de pell. Sempre porto una ampolla d'aigua i un paraigua plegable, perquè a Barcelona de vegades plou. No porto cap ordinador: és massa pesat.
