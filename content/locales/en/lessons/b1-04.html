1. Quin dia? Talking about days

The names of the days, months and seasons are in the lesson “Numbers and the calendar”. Here you’ll learn how to use them in conversation: what day it is today, what happens on which day, when someone’s birthday is.

Quin dia és avui? — What day is it today?
Avui és dimecres. — Today is Wednesday.
I demà? — Demà és dijous. — And tomorrow? — Tomorrow is Thursday.
Quins dies són el cap de setmana? — El dissabte i el diumenge. — Which days are the weekend? — Saturday and Sunday.

Three words you can’t do without: ahir — yesterday, avui — today, demà — tomorrow. “The day after tomorrow” is demà passat, “the day before yesterday” is abans-d'ahir.

1.1 Quin, quina, quins, quines: “which” agrees

English which or what never changes: what day, what time, what classes. In Catalan, quin before a noun changes with the noun’s gender (masculine or feminine) and number, just like an adjective:

SingularPlural
masculineQuin dia és avui?Quins dies treballes?
feminineQuina hora és?Quines classes tens avui?

Watch out

Not Què dia és avui? but Quin dia és avui? Què means “what” on its own, with no noun after it: Què fas dilluns? — What are you doing on Monday?

1.2 Hi ha mercat el dissabte: what happens on which day

To say that something takes place on a certain day, use hi ha (“there is”):

Quin dia hi ha classe de català? — Which day is the Catalan class?
Hi ha classe el dimarts i el dijous. — There’s class on Tuesdays and Thursdays.
A la plaça del barri hi ha mercat els dissabtes. — There’s a market in our neighbourhood square on Saturdays.
El diumenge no hi ha escola. — There’s no school on Sundays.

  • The day takes no preposition. English on Monday pushes you towards a dilluns or en dilluns, but there’s no preposition here.
  • With no article, dilluns is one particular Monday, usually the coming one: Què fas dilluns? — What are you doing on Monday? With the article, el dilluns or els dilluns, it’s every Monday, a habit: El dilluns treballo a casa. — On Mondays I work from home.
  • A range is de … a …: de dilluns a divendres — Monday to Friday. Weekdays as a whole are entre setmana: Entre setmana treballo i el cap de setmana descanso. — I work during the week and rest at the weekend.

Almost every Catalan town has a mercat setmanal — an open-air market once a week, on its own day. Ask your neighbours: Quin dia hi ha mercat aquí?

1.3 Birthdays

Quan és el teu aniversari? — When is your birthday?
El meu aniversari és el 2 de febrer. — My birthday is on 2 February.
És a l'hivern. — It’s in winter.
Per molts anys! — Happy birthday!

A date takes the article: el 2 de febrer, el 23 d'abril. The birthday wish Per molts anys! literally means “for many years”. And aniversari means both “birthday” and “anniversary”.

2. Esmorzo, dino, sopo: mealtimes

The unit’s title, A l'hivern sopo a les nou, means “In winter I have dinner at nine”. For Catalans that’s early: in summer dinner is at ten. All meals are an hour or two later than in Britain or the US.

MealVerbWhenIn English
l'esmorzaresmorzar7–9 a.m., and again at 10–11breakfast

el berenarberenar5–6 p.m., a snack, especially for childrenafternoon snack
el soparsopar9–10 p.m.dinner, supper

The noun and the verb are the same word: el sopar — dinner, sopar — to have dinner. English needs “have”: have lunch; Catalan has one verb for each meal. The general word for a meal is l'àpat.

False friends

Dinar looks like dinner, but it’s lunch, the big midday meal. Dinner is sopar — which isn’t soup either: soup is la sopa. Hence the tongue-twister La Marta sopa sopa — “Marta has soup for dinner”. And money is els diners.

2.1 -ar verbs in the present

All four verbs follow the most common pattern, verbs ending in -ar. English has one form for almost everyone (I eat, you eat, we eat); Catalan has six, and the ending alone tells you who is doing it, so the subject (jo, tu…) is usually left out: Sopo a les nou — I have dinner at nine.

  • The unstressed -o of “I” sounds like [u]: sopo [ˈsopu], dino [ˈdinu]. Unstressed -es, -en have the neutral vowel of English about: sopes [ˈsopəs]. So sopa and sopes differ only by the final s.
  • Same pattern: berenar (bereno, berenes, berena…), and also treballar, estudiar, començar, acabar, tancar.

A quina hora dines? — Dino a les dues. — What time do you have lunch? — At two.
Què esmorzes? — Esmorzo un cafè amb llet i una torrada. — What do you have for breakfast? — A white coffee and a piece of toast.
Els nens berenen a les cinc. — The children have their snack at five.
Jo no bereno mai. — I never have an afternoon snack.

If you know Spanish

The endings are close to Spanish, but where Spanish has a, Catalan has e: sopes, sopen — cenas, cenan. And dinar has nothing to do with dinero.

Esmorzar de forquilla

Many people just have a coffee first thing, and the real esmorzar comes at 10–11: a sandwich in a bar during the break. There’s also the tradition of esmorzar de forquilla, a “knife-and-fork breakfast”: on a day off, a mid-morning meal of meat, botifarra sausages and beans.

One more useful phrase: fer la migdiada — to have an after-lunch nap, a siesta.

3. Quina hora és? Time to the nearest five minutes

The quarts system (quarters of the coming hour) is explained in “Numbers and the calendar”. Now for time to the nearest five minutes, parts of the day, and the question “at what time?”.

For English speakers

English counts from the hour that has passed: quarter past seven. Quarts count towards the next hour: how many quarters of the way to eight you are. Un quart de vuit = 7:15, dos quarts de vuit = 7:30, tres quarts de vuit = 7:45. Your ear hears “eight”, but it’s still seven-something. If you say British half eight, careful: that’s 8:30, which in Catalan is dos quarts de nou, not dos quarts de vuit.

3.1 És or són

The verb agrees with what follows it: one hour or one quarter — és, more than one — són. English always says it is; Catalan says “they are two”.

És la una. — It’s one o’clock. Són les dues. — It’s two o’clock.
És un quart de deu. — It’s quarter past nine.
Són dos quarts de deu. — It’s half past nine.
Són tres quarts de deu. — It’s quarter to ten.

3.2 Every five minutes

Between the quarters, add or take away five minutes from the nearest quarter: un quart i cinc — a quarter and five more minutes, dos quarts menys cinc — two quarters minus five. On the right is the same time read “off the clock face”, which you’ll also hear a lot — it works more like English.

  • Falten cinc minuts per a les vuit — “five minutes are missing until eight”, i.e. five to eight. Falta or falten depends on the number: falta un minut, falten deu minuts.
  • Before an hour starting with a vowel, de becomes d': un quart d'una (12:15), dos quarts d'onze (10:30).

3.3 Morning, afternoon or night

Catalans use the 12-hour clock and add the part of the day instead of a.m. and p.m. (there’s a diagram in the calendar lesson):

For English speakers

English three in the morning is not del matí but de la matinada: the small hours after midnight have their own name. Matí starts when people get up. And tarda is longer than afternoon: at seven in the evening it’s still tarda — vespre only begins around dinner time.

3.4 A quina hora? At what time?

“At” a time is a + the time: a la una, a les set, a un quart de nou, a dos quarts de deu.

A quina hora quedem? — A dos quarts de set, davant del cinema. — What time shall we meet? — At half past six, outside the cinema.
Arribo cap a les vuit. — I’ll get there around eight.
La reunió és a les deu en punt. — The meeting is at ten o’clock sharp.
Quina hora tens? — What time do you make it? (literally “What hour do you have?”)

4. Quin horari fa? Opening hours and timetables

You’ll see a sign with opening hours on every door. A neighbourhood library might have one like this:

Biblioteca del barri — Horari
Dillunstancat
De dimarts a divendres10–14 h, 16–20.30 h
Dissabte10–14 h
Diumenges i festiustancat
Horari d'estiu (juliol i agost)Mon–Fri, 9–14 h

How to read it aloud:

El dilluns la biblioteca està tancada. — On Mondays the library is closed.
De dimarts a divendres obre a les deu i tanca a dos quarts de nou del vespre. — Tuesday to Friday it opens at ten and closes at half past eight in the evening.
Al migdia tanca de dues a quatre. — At lunchtime it’s closed from two to four.
A l'estiu fa horari de matí. — In summer it only opens in the morning.

4.1 Useful words

  • obrir — to open: obre — it opens; tancar — to close: tanca — it closes. La botiga obre a les nou. — The shop opens at nine.
  • obert / oberta — open, tancat / tancada — closed. They agree with whatever is open: el bar està obert, la farmàcia està oberta.
  • començar — to start, acabar — to finish: La classe comença a les sis i acaba a les vuit.
  • de … a … — from … to …: de nou a dues, de dilluns a dissabte. Before una and onze: d'onze a una.
  • un festiu — a public holiday; tancat per vacances — closed for holidays (the sign on many doors in August).

Quin és l'horari de la farmàcia? — What are the chemist’s opening hours?
A quina hora tanca el supermercat? — What time does the supermarket close?
Avui és festiu: està tot tancat. — It’s a holiday today: everything is closed.

The lunch break

Many small shops and offices close al migdia, roughly from 2 to 4 or 5 p.m. If a sign shows two time slots, say 9–13.30 h and 17–20 h, don’t turn up in the early afternoon.

4.2 Trains and announcements

Train timetables use the 24-hour clock, and the minutes are read as a plain number. A train at 17.38: El tren surt a les cinc i trenta-vuit or, at the station, a les disset i trenta-vuit. Quarts don’t work for exact minutes like these.

A quina hora passa el tren per Girona? — What time does the train go through Girona?
Passa a les onze i quaranta-dos. — At 11:42.

5. Quin temps fa? The weather

The word temps means both “time” and “weather”. English says it is sunny, it is cold; Catalan uses the verb fer (“to make, to do”): fa sol — literally “it makes sun”.

In CatalanIn EnglishLiterally
Fa sol.It’s sunny.it makes sun
Fa calor.It’s hot.it makes heat
Fa fred.It’s cold.it makes cold
Fa fresca.It’s cool, chilly.it makes coolness
Fa vent.It’s windy.it makes wind
Fa bo.The weather’s nice.it makes good
Fa mal temps.The weather’s bad.it makes bad weather
Plou.It’s raining.it rains
Neva.It’s snowing.it snows
Hi ha boira.It’s foggy.there is fog
Està ennuvolat.It’s cloudy.it is clouded
  • Plou comes from ploure, neva from nevar. “It’s raining right now” is Està plovent. Rain itself is la pluja, snow is la neu.
  • Fa bo, not fa bé: bé is “well”, for how you feel (Estic bé — I’m fine).
  • Fa una mica de fred — it’s a bit cold. For heavy rain people say Plou a bots i barrals — “it’s raining cats and dogs” (literally “by wineskins and casks”).

La calor is feminine

Because calor is a feminine noun, “very hot” is Fa molta calor (“it makes much heat”), not Fa molt calor. El fred is masculine: Fa molt fred. La fresca is feminine too: Fa fresca.

If you know Spanish

Spanish el calor is masculine, so Spanish speakers often slip into fa molt calor. In Catalan it’s la calor.

5.1 Weather through the seasons

Seasons take the article and a: a la primavera, a l'estiu, a la tardor, a l'hivern — in spring, in summer, in autumn, in winter. About Barcelona:

  • A l'estiu fa molta calor i hi ha molta humitat.
  • A la tardor plou bastant i de vegades fa vent.
  • A l'hivern fa fresca, però gairebé no neva mai.
  • A la primavera fa bo i fa sol.

In summer it’s very hot and humid. In autumn it rains quite a lot and is sometimes windy. In winter it’s chilly, but it almost never snows. In spring the weather’s nice and sunny.

That sticky summer heat when you can hardly breathe is la xafogor: Avui fa xafogor. — It’s muggy today. In Barcelona in July and August you’ll need this word every day.

For English speakers

Your classmates will probably ask what the weather is like back home. For example: A Londres plou molt i a l'hivern fa fred. A l'estiu fa bo. Temperatures are in Celsius: “minus five” is cinc graus sota zero.

6. A holiday message

The unit’s final task is a short message to a friend from a trip: where you are, what the weather’s like, what you’re doing. Here’s an example:

Hola, Jordi! Com va? Soc a Cadaqués de vacances amb la meva germana. Fa un temps fantàstic: fa sol i fa molta calor, però a la nit fa fresca. Al matí anem a la platja. Dinem a les tres en un restaurant del port i a la tarda fem la migdiada. Aquí ningú sopa abans de les deu! Demà és dissabte i hi ha mercat a la plaça. T'envio una foto del poble. Una abraçada! Irene
