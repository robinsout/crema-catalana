1. Carrer, plaça, avinguda: kinds of street

The unit opens with A quin carrer vius? — “Which street do you live on?”. To answer, you need the names for different kinds of street:

CatalanAbbreviationIn EnglishNote
el carrerc.streetmasculine: el carrer, un carrer
la plaçapl.squareç is always an [s]: [ˈplasə]
l'avingudaav.avenuefeminine; l' before a vowel
el passeigpg.boulevard, promenadefinal -ig sounds like -tch in fetch: [pəˈsɛtʃ]
la ramblarbla.rambla: a street with a wide pedestrian walkway down the middlenot just in Barcelona: almost every Catalan town has one
la rondarda.ring roadbuilt where the old city walls used to be
el passatgeptge.passage, lanea small street inside a block
  • El carrer is pronounced [kəˈre]: the unstressed a is the neutral vowel of about, and the r at the end of words in -er is usually silent — just like Xavier (lesson 2). The double rr in the middle is a strong rolled r.
  • A neighbourhood is el barri: Visc al barri de Gràcia. On a form, the town or city is la població.
  • La rambla takes a small letter when it’s the kind of street and a capital in a name: la Rambla de Catalunya.

1.1 How a street name is built

English puts the name first: Mallorca Street. Catalan puts the kind of street first and links the name with de (“of”) — like the Street of Mallorca. De merges with the article when there is one:

NameStreetWhat happened
Mallorcael carrer de Mallorcajust de
Aragóel carrer d'Aragóbefore a vowel — d'
la Indústriael carrer de la Indústriade la doesn’t merge
el Nordel carrer del Nordde + el = del
els Àngelsel carrer dels Àngelsde + els = dels
Nou, Major, Granel carrer Nou, el carrer Majoran adjective — no de: “New Street”, “High Street”

In conversation de is often dropped: Visc a Mallorca, 120, el carrer Aragó. In writing, especially on forms and envelopes, keep the de.

Gender of streets

English streets have no gender, so it’s easy to guess wrong. Learn each with its article: el carrer, al carrer, un carrer petit, aquest carrer — masculine, like el passeig. But la plaça, l'avinguda, la rambla are feminine.

If you know Spanish

La calle is feminine, but el carrer is masculine: not la carrer.

2. Quina és la teva adreça? The whole address

The unit’s two key questions and their answers:

A quin carrer vius? — Visc al carrer de Sepúlveda. — Which street do you live on? — On Sepúlveda Street.
Quina és la teva adreça? — Carrer de Sepúlveda, número 152, tercer segona. — What’s your address? — 152 Sepúlveda Street, third floor, second door.
On vius? — Visc a Barcelona, a l'avinguda de Roma. — Where do you live? — In Barcelona, on Avinguda de Roma.

  • L'adreça — “address”, feminine. For an address say l'adreça; la direcció is mainly “direction” or “management”. Notice that the house number comes after the street name, the reverse of English: carrer de Sepúlveda, 152.
  • Quina, not quin: “which” agrees with adreça, a feminine noun. With carrer it’s quin: A quin carrer?
  • The question starts with a: A quin carrer vius? — literally “On which street do you live?”. Catalan never leaves a preposition at the end the way English does (which street do you live on?).
  • The verb viure in every person is in lesson 2, chapter 6. Here you mostly need visc, vius, viu.

2.1 a, al, a la — and suddenly en

English uses three prepositions here: in Barcelona, on Mallorca Street, at number 120. Catalan uses one — a — for towns, streets and squares alike. It merges with the masculine article:

Articlea + articleExample
town, no articleaVisc a Terrassa.
el carrer, el passeigalVisc al carrer Major. Viu al passeig de Sant Joan.
la plaça, la ramblaa laVivim a la plaça del Sol.
l'avingudaa l'Viuen a l'avinguda Diagonal.
els, lesals, a lesViu als afores. Treballa a les Rambles.

But when the street comes with un, una, aquest, algun instead of the definite article, Catalan usually uses en; a is also correct:

Visc en una plaça molt tranquil·la. — I live on a very quiet square.
Vius en un carrer o en una avinguda? — Do you live on a street or on an avenue?
En aquest carrer hi ha molts bars. — There are lots of bars on this street.
but: Visc a la plaça de l'Església. — I live on Church Square.

The rule in one line

a + el / la / l' and town names; en (or a) + un / una / aquest / aquesta. The only mistake is en before the definite article.

If you know Spanish

Spanish always has en here (vivo en la calle Mayor), so the Spanish habit works only in the second half of the rule: Visc en el carrer Major is a mistake — Visc al carrer Major.

2.2 House numbers: up to 999

House numbers on Barcelona’s long streets run into three digits, so you need to say them and catch them by ear. How the hundreds are built is covered in Els nombres i el calendari, chapter 2. In short:

  • A house number is el número, masculine, so you say dos-cents. The form dues-centes is only for feminine nouns: dues-centes persones.
  • In cents the t before the final s is silent: tres-cents sounds like [ˌtɾɛsˈsens].

If you know Spanish

Cent is both cien and ciento. And there are no irregular hundreds: cinc-cents (not quinientos), set-cents, nou-cents.

3. A quin pis vius? Floor and door

In a block of flats, the address adds the floor and the door: 3r 2a reads tercer segona — “third floor, second door”. The full table of ordinals is in Els nombres i el calendari, chapter 3. What you need for addresses:

WrittenRead asMeaning
1r 1aprimer primera1st floor, 1st door
2n 3asegon tercera2nd floor, 3rd door
4t 2aquart segona4th floor, 2nd door
5è 1acinquè primera5th floor, 1st door
6è 4asisè quarta6th floor, 4th door
  • Why is the first word masculine and the second feminine? It’s el tercer pis (“the third floor”) and la segona porta (“the second door”). The words pis and porta themselves are usually dropped.
  • The little letters work like English -st, -nd, -rd, -th: 1r = primer, 1a = primera, 2n = segon, 4t = quart, 5è = cinquè.
  • El pis means both “floor” and “flat”: Visc en un pis petit. — I live in a small flat. Visc al tercer pis. — I live on the third floor.
  • Sometimes doors are marked with letters: 3r B — tercer B.

A quin pis vius? — Al quart. — Which floor do you live on? — The fourth.
I quina porta? — La primera. — And which door? — The first.
Visc al cinquè primera. — I live on the fifth floor, first door.

In older buildings, especially in the Eixample, floor numbers don’t start right above the street:

For English speakers

In British English the ground floor is at street level and the first floor is one up; in American English the first floor is at street level. Barcelona goes further than both: in an old building primer can be the fourth level from the street — baixos, entresol, principal, then primer. If you’re invited to a primer in the Eixample, expect to climb more stairs than you think.

False friends

La llibreria is a bookshop, not a library; to borrow books you go to la biblioteca. La parada is a stop, but also a market stall: la parada de fruita. And el pàrquing is a car park, never a “parking space” on the street.

If you know Spanish

Many are close: sabateria — zapatería, perruqueria — peluquería, llibreria — librería. But a bakery is la fleca or el forn, not panadería, and la botiga is any shop: Spanish bodega in the sense of “cellar” is el celler.

5. Hi ha or és: “there is” and “is (located)”

Saying what’s near your home and asking the way use two different constructions — just like English there is and where is:

What we meanConstructionExample
something exists (new to the listener)hi ha + un, una, a number, no articleAl meu carrer hi ha una fleca i dos bars.
where something known is (with el, la, with a name)ser: és, sónOn és la fleca? La fleca és davant del mercat.

Hi ha una farmàcia a prop d'aquí? — Is there a chemist’s near here?
Sí, n'hi ha una al carrer Major. — Yes, there’s one on Carrer Major.
On és l'Ajuntament? — Where’s the town hall?
És a la plaça, al costat de l'església. — It’s on the square, next to the church.
Què hi ha al teu carrer? — What is there on your street?
No hi ha cap botiga, només cases. — There isn’t a single shop, only houses.

  • Hi ha covers both there is and there are: hi ha una fleca, hi ha tres fleques. Hi han is common in speech, but the standard is hi ha.
  • “There isn’t any / not a single” is no hi ha cap + a singular noun: No hi ha cap banc. — There’s no bank. Note the double negative: no … cap, literally “there isn’t no bank”. Ningú means “nobody”, and ningun is a Castilianism in modern Catalan — say cap.
  • Inside n'hi ha una (“there is one”) hides the little word en — the same as in en tinc dos from the last lesson. For now, just recognise it.

On és, not on està

Catalan has two verbs “to be”, ser and estar. For buildings, streets, towns — anything that doesn’t move — location takes ser: On és el mercat? És al final del carrer. (introduction, chapter 3.3). With people you’ll often hear està (La Maria està a la feina.), but the main form is és: La Maria és a la feina. For addresses and directions, only és.

If you know Spanish

¿Dónde está el mercado? pulls you towards On està el mercat? — say On és el mercat?

6. Davant, darrere, al costat: exactly where

Landmarks follow one pattern: a place word + de + the place, like English in front of, next to. De merges with the article: del mercat, de la farmàcia, de l'escola.

al costat denext to, besideEl banc és al costat de la fleca.

  • Learn davant — darrere as a pair: “in front — behind”. Both start with d-, so they’re easy to mix up. A hook for darrere: its short form rere sounds like English rear.
  • When it’s clear what from, drop de and the place, as in English: Hi ha un caixer aquí al costat. — There’s a cash machine just next door. Viu molt a prop. — She lives very close.
  • A prop is said as one word, [əˈpɾɔp]; lluny [ʎuɲ] starts like million and ends like canyon.
  • A street corner is la cantonada; el racó is a corner inside a room.

6.1 Casa meva és davant de…

The key sentence of the unit’s final task is where your home is. “My home” about where you live drops the article and puts the possessive after: casa meva, as in “at home” — a casa — in lesson 2, chapter 4:

On és casa teva? — Where’s your place?
Casa meva és al costat de l'escola, davant d'un parc. — My place is next to the school, opposite a park.
Casa seva és darrere del mercat. — His (her) place is behind the market.
Visc a prop de casa teva! — I live near you!

Casa meva is “my home, my place”, even if it’s a flat — like English my place. For the building, say el meu edifici; for the flat itself, el meu pis.

For English speakers

English has a word for each relation: of, to, from. Catalan uses de for all of them — in front of, next to, far from — and fuses it with the article. So in every phrase about place, check three things: the place word, de, and the right form of the article — davant del mercat, davant de la farmàcia, davant d'un parc.

7. Envelope, form, card: addresses in writing

In Catalonia an address goes from the street to the town:

Sra. Anna Ferrer Roig
C. de Còrsega, 377, 3r 2a
08037 Barcelona
  • First the kind of street and its name, then a comma and the house number (after the name, unlike English), then the floor and door.
  • 08037 is the postcode, el codi postal. Postcodes in the province of Barcelona start with 08. Aloud, people read it digit by digit: zero vuit, zero tres set.
  • s/n — sense número, “no number”: common for buildings on squares.
  • Sr. — senyor (Mr), Sra. — senyora (Mrs, Ms).

Pronunciation

adreça — [əˈðɾɛsə]: ç is always [s], and a d between vowels is soft, close to th in other. Cognoms — [kuŋˈnɔms]: the gn here is two sounds, as in English signal, not the ny of canyon.

8.8 Describe your neighbourhood

Write five to seven sentences: where you live (street, number, floor), what there is on your street and where it is in relation to your home. Use hi ha, és and at least three place words.

Example

Visc a Barcelona, al barri de Gràcia. La meva adreça és carrer de Verdi, número 96, segon primera. És un carrer petit i tranquil. Al meu carrer hi ha un forn de pa, una fruiteria i un cinema. El forn és al costat de casa meva. La parada de metro és a prop, a la plaça. No hi ha cap supermercat al meu carrer, però n'hi ha un al carrer del costat, darrere del mercat.

Mission

Tell a classmate your address in Catalan — street, number, floor — and where your home is using landmarks: Visc al carrer…, número…, tercer segona. Casa meva és davant de…, al costat de… Then ask them: A quin carrer vius? Què hi ha a prop de casa teva? — and write down their answer as if filling in a form.
