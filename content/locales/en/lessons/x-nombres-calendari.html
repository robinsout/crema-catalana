1.1 One and two have a gender

English numbers never change. In Catalan, 1 and 2 agree with the noun, because every noun is masculine or feminine:

  • When counting you say u: u, dos, tres. Before a noun — un llibre (a book), una casa (a house).
  • dos cafès (two coffees), but dues cerveses (two beers).
  • The gender stays in longer numbers too: vint-i-una persones (21 people), trenta-dues taules (32 tables), dues-centes pàgines (200 pages).

1.2 Pronunciation

  • cinc [siŋ] — the final c is barely heard, like the end of sing.
  • nou [nɔw] and deu [dɛw] — open vowels; vuit [bujt] — one syllable, starting with a b sound.
  • onze, dotze, tretze, catorze, quinze, setze — the z and tz are voiced: [z] as in zoo, [dz] as in adze.
  • disset, divuit, dinou — originally “ten and seven”, “ten and eight”, “ten and nine”; two syllables, stress on the last.

2.1 Building numbers: where the hyphens go

  • 21–29: with -i- (“and”) — vint-i-u, vint-i-dos, vint-i-nou. Only the twenties!
  • 31–99: just a hyphen, as in English forty-five — trenta-u, quaranta-cinc, noranta-nou.
  • Hundreds: a hyphen between the units and cents — tres-cents; then a space: tres-cents quaranta-dos (342). No “and” as in British three hundred and forty-two.
  • Thousands: mil never changes and stands apart — dos mil, vint mil. Not “un mil”: 1,000 is just mil.

Catalan writes the decimal point as a comma and groups thousands with a full stop or a space: 1.500 is fifteen hundred, 2,5 is two and a half.

2.2 Years

Years are said as one whole number, never split in two like twenty twenty-six:

dos mil vint-i-sis — 2026
mil nou-cents noranta-dos — 1992 (the Barcelona Olympics)
mil set-cents catorze — 1714 (remembered on the Diada, 11 September)

From fifth on, they all follow one pattern: -è / -ena. Like English 1st, 2nd, 4th, the short form repeats the end of the word: 1r — primer, 2n — segon, 4t — quart. The feminine always ends in -a.

In everyday life

This is how addresses give the floor and the door: c/ Verdi, 12, 3r 2a is read tercer segona — third floor, second door. Floors are counted European style: the floor above the ground floor (planta baixa) is the first.

4. Telling the time

Quina hora és? — What time is it?
És la una. — It’s one o’clock. Són les dues. — It’s two o’clock.
A quina hora quedem? — What time shall we meet? A les vuit. — At eight.

Hours are feminine (la una, les dues) because hora is. Timetables use the 24-hour clock: a les vint is 8 pm.

4.1 The «quarts» system

English counts from the hour that has passed: quarter past two. Catalans often count towards the next hour: how many quarters of the way to three o’clock you already are.

The shaded part of the clock is how many quarters have passed on the way to three.

The trap

Your ear hears “two” in 2:15, but the Catalan names three: un quart de tres is “one quarter of (the way to) three”. And if you’re used to British half two (2:30), note that dos quarts de tres is the same time.

The borders are rough: tarda lasts as long as it’s light, and migdia is lunchtime, not just 12:00 sharp.

The day is cut differently from English. La tarda covers the whole afternoon and early evening: at 7 pm, when an English speaker says good evening, Barcelona still says Bona tarda!

7.1 Months

  • Written with a small letter, unlike in English. Most look like their English names; the odd ones out are gener (January), maig (May), juny (June), juliol (July) and agost (August).
  • Before a month starting with a vowel, de becomes d': d'abril, d'agost, d'octubre, but de gener, de març.
  • “In March”: al març or el mes de març.

7.2 Seasons

With the article and a — literally “at the spring”: a la primavera, a l'estiu, a la tardor, a l'hivern. Unit 4 is named just like that: A l'hivern sopo a les nou. — In winter I have dinner at nine.

7.3 The date

The day comes first, then the month, joined by de: “3 of October”.

Quin dia és avui? — What’s the date today?
Avui és dissabte, 3 d'octubre de 2026.
Som a 3 d'octubre. — It’s the 3rd of October.
El meu aniversari és el 15 de maig. — My birthday is on 15 May.

Written short, the day goes first too: 3/10/2026 is 3 October, never 10 March.
