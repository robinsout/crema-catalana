1. The map of tenses

Most Catalan tenses have a rough English partner, but the borders between them are drawn differently. Two things matter most: the everyday past vaig parlar looks like “I go to speak” but means I spoke, and he parlat (“I have spoken”) is used for everything that happened today. 2. How to read the map

2.1 Three pasts: where the borders are

Three neighbouring tenses share the past, and choosing between them is the key skill of this topic.

TenseAsk yourselfExample
PerfetIs the time period still going on? (today, this week, this year, “already”, “never”)Avui he treballat molt.
I worked a lot today.
Passat perifràsticDid it happen in a period that is over? (yesterday, last year, in 2019)Ahir vaig treballar molt.
I worked a lot yesterday.
ImperfetIs it background, a description or a habit, rather than one event?Abans treballava molt.
I used to work a lot.

For English speakers

The perfet/perifràstic border is close to the English rule “finished time → simple past”: I worked yesterday, but I’ve worked this week. The difference is today: Catalan treats today as unfinished, so This morning I had breakfast at home is Aquest matí he esmorzat a casa, even in the evening.

And English has no single imperfet. Each of these is parlava in Catalan: I used to speak, I would speak (every day), I was speaking, and a plain I spoke when it describes how things were.

2.2 An event against a background

The imperfet sets the scene, the perifràstic moves the story on — like English was …ing and the simple past:

Plovia quan vaig sortir de casa. — It was raining when I left home.
Mentre sopàvem, va sonar el telèfon. — While we were having dinner, the phone rang.

2.3 The past before the past, the future before the future

Compound tenses place an event before another point on the timeline, just as in English:

Quan vaig arribar, la classe ja havia començat. — When I arrived, the class had already started.
A les vuit ja hauré sortit de la feina. — By eight I will have left work.

3. One story on the timeline

One life story, told with every tense in order:

  1. Before moving Plusquamperfet

    Abans de venir a Barcelona, ja havia estudiat castellà.

    Before coming to Barcelona, I had already studied Spanish.

  2. 2019 Perifràstic

    L'any 2019 vaig arribar a Barcelona.

    In 2019 I arrived in Barcelona.

  3. The first years Imperfet

    Vivia a Gràcia i treballava cada dia fins tard.

    I lived in Gràcia and worked late every day.

  4. This year Perfet

    Aquest any he començat a estudiar català.

    This year I started learning Catalan.

  5. Now Present

    Ara estudio català dos cops per setmana.

    Now I study Catalan twice a week.

  6. This minute Estar + gerundi

    En aquest moment estic llegint aquesta lliçó.

    Right now I’m reading this lesson.

  7. Next summer Futur

    L'estiu que ve parlaré amb els veïns en català.

    Next summer I’ll speak Catalan with my neighbours.

  8. By June Futur perfet

    Al juny ja hauré acabat el Bàsic 3.

    By June I’ll have finished Bàsic 3.

Note the English: I lived, I started — plain past both times, while Catalan uses the imperfet for the background and the perfet for “this year”. And an imagined branch, off the line:

Si tingués més temps, estudiaria cada dia. — If I had more time, I would study every day.

4.1 How it works

  • Imperfet: -ar verbs end in -ava, all the others in -ia: perdia, dormia, servia.
  • Perifràstic: only the helper (a form of anar, “to go”) changes; the verb itself stays in the infinitive. In the plural there are equally correct long forms vàrem, vàreu, varen.
  • Futur and condicional: one word, not “will” or “would” + verb — the endings are added to the infinitive. Verbs in -re lose the final -e: perdre → perdré, viure → viuré.
  • All compound tenses are haver + participle, like English have + participle. Only the tense of haver changes:

6. Passat simple: recognise it, don’t use it

Catalan also has a one-word past, like English spoke. In everyday central Catalan vaig parlar has replaced it, but you’ll meet it all the time in books, the news and Wikipedia, mostly in the third person.

Common irregular forms worth recognising: fou (= va ser, was), tingué (= va tenir, had), digué (= va dir, said), nasqué (= va néixer, was born).

L'escriptora nasqué a Barcelona i fou professora. — The writer was born in Barcelona and was a teacher.

7. Subjunctive and imperative: a quick look

English has almost lost its subjunctive (it survives in if I were you), but Catalan uses it every day: after wishes, after quan about the future, in “if” sentences. We’ll go into it in Bàsic 2–3; for now, learn to recognise the forms.

7.1 The imperative

Positive forms: parla (you, informal), parli (you, polite), parlem (let’s), parleu (you all), parlin (you all, polite). Where English just adds don’t, a negative command switches to the subjunctive: No parlis! No parleu! — Don’t speak!

8. Traps

  • Aquest matí vaig anar al mercat. → Aquest matí he anat al mercat. “This morning I went” is the perfet in Catalan: today isn’t over.
  • Ahir he anat al cinema. → Ahir vaig anar al cinema. With “yesterday”, “last year” and dates, use the perifràstic.
  • “I have lived here for two years” — Fa dos anys que visc aquí. (“it makes two years that I live here”). Where English uses the present perfect for something still going on, Catalan uses the present.
  • Don’t mix up vaig (I go) and vaig + infinitive (I did): Vaig a la platja. — I’m going to the beach. Vaig anar a la platja. — I went to the beach. For “I’m going to eat”, use Ara menjo. or Menjaré ara.: Vaig a menjar is borrowed from Spanish and counts as incorrect.
  • The participle agrees with la and les placed before the verb: Has vist la Maria? Sí, l'he vista. — Have you seen Maria? Yes, I’ve seen her.
